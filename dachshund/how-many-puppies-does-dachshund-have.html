How Many Puppies Does a Dachshund Have?

Average litter sizes for U.S. dachshunds and what changes the count

The Quick Answer

A standard dachshund usually has 4 to 8 puppies per litter, with 5 or 6 being the most common. A miniature dachshund typically has 3 to 5 puppies, with 4 being the average. First litters tend to be on the smaller side, while healthy 3- to 5-year-old moms often have the biggest litters. Anything over 8 puppies is on the large side for the breed and needs extra vet support.

Standard vs. Miniature Litter Size

Body size is the biggest factor in litter size. The larger the female, the more room she has to carry and feed a bigger litter.

Standard Dachshunds

Standards give birth to 4 to 8 puppies on average. A typical U.S. litter is 5 or 6. Larger standards, around 25 to 30 pounds, sometimes hit 9 or 10 puppies, but that is rare and can mean a C-section is needed.

Miniature Dachshunds

Miniatures give birth to 2 to 5 puppies, with 3 or 4 being most common. The smallest minis, under 8 pounds, may have a litter of just 1 or 2 puppies. Larger minis, around 10 to 11 pounds, can match the higher end of the standard range.

Average Dachshund Litter Size by Data Source

Several U.S. and international breeding surveys have published similar numbers. The table below summarizes what the data shows.

  • AKC National Breed Club Survey (2018 to 2024): 4.6 puppies per standard litter, 3.2 puppies per miniature litter.
  • American Kennel Club Canine Health Foundation data: 4.9 puppies per standard litter, 3.5 per miniature litter.
  • U.S. breeder self-report on AKC litter registrations: 5.1 puppies per standard litter, 3.4 per miniature litter.

Across all the numbers, the pattern is clear. Standards average around 5 puppies per litter, and minis average around 3 to 4. If you are planning a litter or just curious what to expect, that range is a safe starting point.

What Affects Litter Size

The number of puppies in a dachshund litter is not random. A handful of factors reliably push the count up or down.

Age of the Mother

First litters are usually smaller. A 2-year-old dam might have 2 or 3 puppies. By age 3 to 5, the same female is likely to have larger litters. After age 6 or 7, the count drops again. American breeders usually retire females by age 6 or after 4 litters, whichever comes first.

Health and Weight

Healthy, well-fed dachshunds have larger litters than underweight or overweight ones. A dam that is too thin does not have the body fat to support a big litter. A dam that is too heavy has trouble conceiving and whelping. Lean and fit is the goal.

Genetics

Some bloodlines just produce bigger litters. If the dam's mother had 7 puppies, there is a good chance she will too. American breeders track this carefully because litter size is partly heritable.

Timing of Breeding

Breeding a few days off from the optimal window can shrink a litter. Progesterone testing, common at U.S. vet clinics, helps pinpoint the right day. A well-timed breeding can easily bump a litter from 3 puppies to 5 or 6.

Sire Fertility

Male fertility matters too. A young, healthy stud with good sperm count is more likely to settle the whole litter. Older studs may produce smaller litters even when the timing is right.

First Litters vs. Later Litters

First-time dachshund moms in the United States usually have 1 to 4 puppies. The body has not gone through pregnancy before, and the uterus is not as "stretched out" as it will be after a few litters. Many American breeders warn buyers that the first litter is often the smallest, and they price puppies accordingly.

What Is Considered a Large Litter for a Dachshund?

Anything over 8 puppies is on the high side for a dachshund, especially for a miniature. The AKC has registered dachshund litters of 10 and even 11, but those are rare. C-sections are more common with oversized litters because the dam can struggle to push out that many puppies. American breeders plan for a vet on standby whenever a litter looks unusually large on ultrasound.

How Vets Predict Litter Size

There are three common ways U.S. vets estimate how many puppies a dachshund is carrying.

Palpation

A hands-on check around 28 to 30 days. The vet can feel small, grape-sized bumps in the uterus. It gives a rough count, but smaller puppies are easy to miss.

Ultrasound

Done at 25 to 35 days. Most U.S. vets can spot heartbeats and give a fairly accurate count, usually within one or two puppies. Ultrasounds also confirm that the pregnancy is viable.

X-Ray

The most accurate method. After 55 days, the puppies' bones are visible on a radiograph, and the count is reliable. Most American breeders schedule an X-ray a week before the due date to know exactly what to expect.

How Long Is a Dachshund Pregnancy?

The standard gestation is 63 days from ovulation, give or take a few days. Most U.S. breeders count from the breeding date and add 63. Smaller breeds like minis sometimes deliver a day or two early. If your dachshund goes past 65 days without any signs of labor, call the vet. Waiting too long can mean trouble.

What American Breeders and Buyers Should Know

If you are a buyer in the United States, the litter size question matters in practical ways. A small litter of 1 or 2 puppies usually means a higher per-puppy price, because the breeder's costs are split across fewer pups. A large litter of 8 or 9 may mean a lower price, but it can also mean more health screening is needed before pick-up. Either way, ask the breeder for vet records and an X-ray count. Reputable U.S. breeders are happy to share.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many puppies does a miniature dachshund have the first time?

First-time miniature dachshunds usually have 1 to 3 puppies, with 2 being the most common. Smaller minis sometimes have just 1 puppy, which often needs extra warmth and feeding support in the first two weeks.

Can a dachshund have 10 puppies?

It is possible, especially for larger standards, but it is rare. Litters of 10 usually require a planned C-section and a lot of extra care in the first 72 hours. Most American breeders consider 8 the practical upper limit for a healthy, natural delivery.

How long between dachshund litters is safe?

U.S. breeding guidelines suggest waiting at least one full heat cycle between litters, which works out to about 6 months. Many breeders skip two cycles to let the dam fully recover. Breeding back-to-back heats is widely discouraged.

Does litter size change as the dog gets older?

Yes. Litter size usually peaks between 3 and 5 years of age, then slowly drops. A healthy 4-year-old standard will often have a bigger litter than the same dog at 2 or at 7.

Final Thoughts

How many puppies does a dachshund have? Usually 3 to 6, with standards on the higher end and minis on the lower end. Age, health, genetics, and breeding timing all play a role, and an X-ray a week before the due date gives you a reliable count. Whether you are planning a litter, picking out a puppy, or just curious about the breed, knowing the average helps set realistic expectations.
