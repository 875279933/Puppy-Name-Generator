What Is the Life Expectancy of a Dachshund?

A straightforward look at how long dachshunds live in the United States and what helps them reach a ripe old age

The Quick Answer

Most dachshunds in the United States live between 12 and 16 years, with the average sitting right around 12.7 years. Miniatures often live a year or two longer than standards, and many American owners report their dogs reaching 15, 16, or even 17 with the right care. That is a long stretch for a small dog, and one of the reasons dachshund puppy names keep trending on every puppy name list in the country.

Average Dachshund Lifespan by Size

Size matters more than most people think. AKC registration data and veterinary studies both show that smaller dachshunds tend to outlive the larger ones.

Miniature Dachshund

Miniatures usually weigh under 11 pounds at maturity. Because of their smaller frame, they often live 14 to 17 years. It is not unusual to meet a 15-year-old mini at a vet's waiting room in California, Florida, or New York.

Standard Dachshund

Standards typically weigh between 16 and 32 pounds. They usually live 12 to 14 years, though many reach 15 with proper weight control and joint care. The slightly larger body puts more stress on the spine, which is one reason minis edge out standards in the longevity game.

Rabbit Dachshund (Kaninchen)

Less common in the United States, the rabbit-sized dachshund sometimes reaches 16 years or more. They are recognized in Europe but rarely seen in American show rings.

Does Coat Type Change the Numbers?

Coat type does not have a big impact on lifespan. Smooth, longhaired, and wirehaired dachshunds all fall into the same general range. What does change things is the coat-related health issues that come with each. For example, longhairs sometimes trap moisture in their ears, which can lead to infections if not cleaned. Wirehairs may develop skin allergies that affect comfort in older years.

Health Issues That Affect Dachshund Lifespan

Two health problems take more years off a dachshund's life than anything else. Knowing what they are early can add real years to your dog.

Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD)

About 1 in 4 dachshunds will deal with some form of IVDD in their lifetime. The long spine and short rib cage make this breed uniquely prone to back issues. Stairs, jumping off the couch, and extra body weight all raise the risk. American vets recommend ramps, weight control, and crate rest during recovery.

Obesity

Studies from U.S. veterinary clinics list dachshunds as one of the most commonly overweight breeds. Even a few extra pounds push hard on that long back. Keeping your dog lean is the single biggest thing an owner can do to extend life.

Dental Disease

Small dogs tend to lose teeth early. By age 5, many dachshunds already have some tartar buildup. Without cleanings, dental disease can spread to the heart and kidneys. Annual dental work under anesthesia, plus daily brushing at home, is now standard advice in American vet clinics.

How to Help Your Dachshund Live Longer

There is no magic formula, but American dachshund owners who report the longest-lived dogs tend to follow a few simple habits.

  • Keep the weight down. You should be able to feel the ribs without pressing hard.
  • Skip the stairs when possible. Ramps beat stairs every time for this breed.
  • Feed a measured diet. Free feeding leads to a chunky doxie. Two meals a day works well.
  • Schedule yearly vet visits. After age 7, twice-yearly checkups catch problems earlier.
  • Brush the teeth. A few times a week makes a real difference.
  • Stay active but careful. Short walks and gentle play keep the muscles that support the spine.

What American Owners Report

Across forums like Reddit's r/dachshund and Facebook groups with thousands of U.S. members, the most common ages mentioned for dachshunds that have passed are 13, 14, and 15. Dogs that make it past 16 are usually described as "still healthy" or "slowing down but happy." Many owners say the last two years are the calmest and most affectionate, which is something first-time dachshund owners rarely expect.

Comparing Dachshund Lifespan to Other Popular Breeds

For context, here is how dachshunds stack up against other breeds Americans love:

  • Dachshund: 12 to 16 years
  • Beagle: 12 to 15 years
  • Labrador Retriever: 10 to 12 years
  • Boxer: 10 to 12 years
  • Great Dane: 7 to 10 years

Frequently Asked Questions

Do male or female dachshunds live longer?

There is no strong evidence in U.S. data that one sex outlives the other. Spayed females and neutered males tend to live a bit longer than intact dogs, mostly because of lower risks of certain cancers and infections.

What is the oldest dachshund on record?

A dachshund named Rocky from California was reported to have lived 25 years, though most vets treat that as an outlier. A 20- to 22-year-old doxie is rare but not unheard of in the United States.

Is 10 years old for a dachshund?

10 is past middle age but not old. Many dachshunds stay playful and active into their early teens, especially minis. If your dog is 10, expect several more good years with the right care.
