How Big Does a Schnauzer Get?

Full size range for Miniature, Standard, and Giant Schnauzers, with growth charts and what to expect

← Back to Schnauzer Dog Names

The Short Answer: 11 to 27.5 Inches Depending on Variety

American owners who ask "how big does a schnauzer get" usually want a number, but schnauzers are a bit unusual. They come in three distinct sizes - Miniature, Standard, and Giant - and each one has its own size range recognized by the American Kennel Club. Miniature Schnauzers top out around 14 inches and 20 pounds, Standards reach 19.5 inches and up to 45 pounds, and Giants stretch to 27.5 inches and 95 pounds. So the answer really depends on which schnauzer you bring home.

Schnauzer Size at a Glance

Here's a quick comparison American families often find helpful when deciding which variety fits their home:

  • Miniature Schnauzer: 12 to 14 inches tall, 11 to 20 pounds. The most popular schnauzer in the US.
  • Standard Schnauzer: 17.5 to 19.5 inches tall, 30 to 45 pounds. The original German farm dog.
  • Giant Schnauzer: 23.5 to 27.5 inches tall, 55 to 95 pounds. The largest and most powerful variety.

Miniature Schnauzer Size

The Miniature Schnauzer is the smallest of the three and by far the most common in American homes. They're sturdy little dogs with a big-dog attitude, which is part of the reason they rank in the top 20 most popular breeds in the United States.

Height and Weight

According to AKC breed standards, a Miniature Schnauzer stands 12 to 14 inches at the shoulder. Healthy adults usually weigh between 11 and 20 pounds, with males on the heavier end of the range and females on the lighter side. Most American pet owners find their mini settles around 14 to 17 pounds once they're full grown.

When Do Minis Stop Growing?

Miniature Schnauzers usually reach their full height by 9 to 12 months and fill out their final weight by 12 to 14 months. Some keep slowly adding muscle until they're about 18 months old. If you're thinking about how many puppies your mini might have, the dam's adult size is a good predictor of puppy size at maturity.

Standard Schnauzer Size

The Standard Schnauzer is the original variety, developed in Germany in the 14th or 15th century as an all-purpose farm dog. Standards are medium-sized and athletic, big enough to be a working dog but small enough for many American homes.

Height and Weight

AKC breed standard puts Standards at 17.5 to 19.5 inches at the shoulder for females and 18.5 to 19.5 inches for males. Weight usually falls between 30 and 45 pounds, with a healthy adult often landing right around 35 to 40 pounds. Standards feel solid in your arms but aren't too big for a sedan or a city apartment.

When Do Standards Stop Growing?

Standards take a bit longer to mature than minis. They usually hit their adult height by 12 to 14 months and keep filling out until they're 18 to 24 months old. A good rule of thumb in American households: if your standard is still gaining weight past 18 months, slow down the food and add some exercise - a chunky standard puts extra stress on the joints.

Giant Schnauzer Size

Giant Schnauzers are exactly what they sound like - a much larger, working-line version of the Standard. They were bred in the Bavarian Alps to drive cattle and guard breweries, and they've kept that big, powerful build.

Height and Weight

AKC standards put Giant Schnauzers at 23.5 to 27.5 inches at the shoulder, with males noticeably larger than females. Weight usually lands between 55 and 95 pounds, and a healthy adult often tops out around 70 to 85 pounds. The biggest males can hit 100 pounds, but most American pet owners aim for a lean 75 to 80.

When Do Giants Stop Growing?

Giants are the slowest to mature. They usually reach adult height by 14 to 18 months and don't fully fill out their frame until they're 2 to 3 years old. Keep growth slow and steady - giant breeds that grow too fast often develop joint issues, so most American vets recommend keeping puppies on large-breed puppy food until they're 12 to 18 months old.

Schnauzer Growth Chart by Variety

This rough chart is what most American breeders and vets go by. Individual puppies vary, but it's a good benchmark.

  • Miniature Schnauzer: 2 lbs at 8 weeks, 6 to 8 lbs at 12 weeks, 10 to 14 lbs at 6 months, 11 to 20 lbs at 12 months.
  • Standard Schnauzer: 7 to 10 lbs at 8 weeks, 15 to 20 lbs at 12 weeks, 25 to 35 lbs at 6 months, 30 to 45 lbs at 12 months.
  • Giant Schnauzer: 15 to 25 lbs at 8 weeks, 30 to 40 lbs at 12 weeks, 50 to 70 lbs at 6 months, 55 to 95 lbs at 18 to 24 months.

How to Tell How Big Your Schnauzer Puppy Will Get

Most American breeders can give you a good estimate by 8 weeks. The two most reliable methods:

  • Look at the parents. The dam and sire give you the most accurate forecast. If both parents are on the smaller side of standard, the puppies probably will be too.
  • Use the 16-week rule. A healthy schnauzer is usually about 75% of their adult weight at 4 months. Multiply their 16-week weight by 1.1 to 1.3 to estimate adult weight.

Of course, your vet's growth chart is the most accurate tool. Bring your puppy in for regular weigh-ins and your vet can flag any growth issues early.

Which Schnauzer Size Is Right for Your Home?

Picking the right variety matters more than most first-time owners realize. Here are some common American household scenarios and what tends to fit best:

  • Apartment or small home: Miniature Schnauzer. They adapt well to small spaces as long as they get daily walks and playtime.
  • Family with kids and a yard: Standard Schnauzer. Sturdy, playful, and not too big to handle on a walk.
  • Active home with experience: Giant Schnauzer. They need firm training, lots of space, and an owner who isn't intimidated by a 90-pound dog.

Final Thoughts on Schnauzer Size

How big does a schnauzer get? Anywhere from 11 pounds to 95 pounds, depending on the variety. All three sizes share the same loyal, smart, bearded personality - just in different packaging. Pick the variety that matches your home, your activity level, and how much dog food you want to buy each month.
