Are Newfoundland Smart?

How newfies rank in canine intelligence, what they learn fast, and what to expect when training one in the US

The Short Answer

Yes, newfoundlands are smart, but not in the way a Border Collie or Poodle is smart. Newfies score in the average to above-average range on standard canine intelligence tests, and they rank roughly in the middle of Stanley Coren's working intelligence list. What they lack in speed-of-learning, they make up for in emotional intelligence, problem-solving, and the calm decision-making that made them famous water rescue dogs. For an American family, the practical answer to are newfoundland smart is that they are smart enough to be trainable, smart enough to read a room, and smart enough to save a life in the water.

What Kind of Smart Is a Newfoundland?

Most people who ask are newfoundland smart are thinking about obedience and tricks. Newfies are not built for that. They were bred to make decisions on their own in rough water, to pull a fishing net back to shore, and to keep a watchful eye on a child without being told. That is a different kind of intelligence than a Border Collie, but it is real and useful.

Three Kinds of Newfoundland Intelligence

  • Working intelligence: Average on Coren's rankings. Newfies learn a new command in 25 to 40 repetitions, compared to 5 for a Border Collie. They obey a known command on the first try about 50% of the time.
  • Emotional intelligence: Well above average. Newfies are famous for reading human mood, calming anxious kids, and sensing when a person needs company.
  • Instinctive intelligence: High. The water rescue instinct is hardwired, and most newfies will try to help a struggling swimmer without any training at all.

What Newfoundlands Learn Quickly

Even though newfies are not the fastest learners in the dog world, there are several things they tend to pick up almost without trying. American trainers and breeders report the same patterns again and again.

1. House Manners and Potty Routines

Most newfies pick up house training surprisingly fast because they want to be near their people and do not like making a mess in their own space. A consistent schedule, frequent trips outside, and a clear door routine usually get a newfie puppy house-trained in 6 to 10 weeks.

2. Walking on a Loose Leash

Newfoundlands are pulling dogs by history, so a 150-pound newfie dragging an owner down the sidewalk is no joke. The good news is that they respond well to gentle, consistent leash pressure. Most US trainers report that a newfie learns loose-leash walking within a month of steady work, especially with a front-clip harness and small treats.

3. Soft Mouth Retrieval

Newfies have famously soft mouths. A newfie can carry an egg without cracking it, and they learn fetch and retrieve games very quickly because the behavior is close to what they were bred to do. Many newfies will bring a specific toy to a specific family member on cue after only a few sessions.

4. Water Rescue Basics

Even without training, a newfie will often jump in to help a swimmer who is splashing hard. The Italian Newfoundland Water Rescue School uses this instinct as the foundation of a formal test, and many US newfies pass basic water work without ever being taught the steps.

Where Newfoundlands Struggle

It would be misleading to answer are newfoundland smart with a flat yes. There are real areas where newfies lag behind the more "obedient" breeds.

1. Repetitive Trick Training

Newfies get bored with repetition fast. Ten minutes of "sit, down, sit, down" and a newfie will lie down and stop cooperating. Short, playful sessions work much better than long drill sessions.

2. Speed and Precision

Do not expect a newfie to run an agility course the way a Border Collie or Australian Shepherd does. Their size, heavy bone, and laid-back attitude work against fast, tight turns. Newfies can do agility, but at their own pace.

3. Independent Problem-Solving on Command

Newfies were bred to think for themselves in the water, but in the living room, they tend to wait for the owner to give clear direction. They are not pushy about figuring things out, which can be mistaken for low intelligence when it is really just a calm, cooperative style.

How to Train a Smart Newfoundland

Because of the way a newfie's mind works, the right training approach matters a lot. The wrong method will make a newfie shut down. The right method will make a newfie look like a genius.

1. Use Food and Praise Together

Newfies are food-motivated but even more people-motivated. The best US trainers use small, soft treats paired with calm verbal praise. A newfie that is told, "Good boy, good job," in a warm voice will work harder for the words than for the treat.

2. Keep Sessions Short

Five to ten minutes is plenty. Newfies have a soft temperament and a long body that gets sore lying on hard floors for training. Two or three short sessions a day beat one long one.

3. Lean into the Breed's Instincts

Instead of fighting the newfie's nature, build around it. Pull a cart, carry a backpack on walks, do a water session at the lake, or play gentle retrieve. The answer to are newfoundland smart becomes obvious when the dog is allowed to do what it was bred to do.

4. Use a Calm Voice

Newfies are sensitive. A loud voice or a sharp leash correction will set training back days. Calm, consistent cues build trust, and a newfie that trusts its owner will try much harder.

5. Socialize Early and Often

Between 3 and 14 weeks is the prime window for a newfie puppy to meet new people, dogs, and surfaces. Newfoundlands that miss this window can become shy or reactive, which is often mistaken for low intelligence when it is really a confidence gap.

How Newfoundlands Compare to Other Smart Breeds

For a fair answer to are newfoundland smart, it helps to see the breed next to dogs people often compare it to.

  • Border Collie: Top of the working intelligence list. Learns a new command in under 5 reps, obeys on the first try 95% of the time. Built for repetition and precision.
  • Poodle: Second on the list. Quick learners, high energy, easy to train for obedience and tricks.
  • German Shepherd: Top-five working intelligence. Strong, athletic, easy to train for protection and service work.
  • Golden Retriever: Top-five working intelligence. Easy to train, motivated, used widely as service dogs.
  • Newfoundland: Middle of the working intelligence list. Slower to learn but extremely trainable for the right owner, with high emotional and instinctive intelligence.

Putting a newfie in a Border Collie training class will not end well. Putting a newfie in a calm, reward-based class with a patient trainer is a totally different story.

What American Owners and Trainers Say

Talk to a newfie owner in the US and you will usually hear the same thing. The dog is smart in the ways that matter at home.

  • "My newfie isn't a trick dog, but she knows exactly when somebody in the house is sad." — Lauren, Illinois
  • "He figured out how to open the back door with his paw at 18 months. We had to childproof the house." — Greg, Washington
  • "Our trainer said she's the smartest 'dumb' dog she's ever met. She just doesn't show off." — Tanya, Georgia

Final Thoughts on Newfoundland Intelligence

The honest answer to are newfoundland smart is yes, but in a newfie way. They are not the dog for someone who wants a Border Collie that can run an agility course. They are the dog for someone who wants a thoughtful, calm, intuitive companion that learns the important things quickly and bonds deeply with the family. Train with patience, lean into the breed's instincts, and the newfie's intelligence shows up in a hundred small ways every day.
