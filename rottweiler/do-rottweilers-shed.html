Do Rottweilers Shed?

Honest answer on rottweiler shedding, seasonal blowouts, and the grooming routine that keeps your home clean

The Short Answer: Yes, Rottweilers Shed - A Lot

Yes, rottweilers shed. They have a short double coat that drops hair year-round and blows out twice a year in spring and fall. Most American rottweiler owners describe the shedding as moderate to heavy - not as bad as a husky or german shepherd, but way more than a bichon or a poodle. If you're considering a rottweiler and dog hair on your clothes, furniture, and car is a deal-breaker, this is a breed you need to plan around. With the right routine, though, the shedding is very manageable.

Why Rottweilers Shed So Much

The rottweiler has a double coat that was designed for the cold German winters of the 1800s. The outer coat is medium-length, coarse, and water-resistant, and the undercoat is soft and dense. The undercoat is what does most of the shedding - it's a thick insulating layer that the dog grows in for winter and sheds out in spring, with a smaller blowout in fall.

Even with no seasonal change triggering a blowout, rottweilers drop individual hairs from both coats year-round. A 100-pound rottweiler is a lot of dog, and a lot of dog means a lot of hair.

How Much Shedding Is Normal

Here's what American rottweiler owners typically experience:

Year-Round Shedding

Expect a steady, light to moderate stream of short black hairs on your clothes, furniture, and floors. It's most noticeable on light-colored fabric and car seats. A quick daily brush and a vacuum with a pet-hair attachment keeps it under control.

Seasonal Blowouts

Twice a year - usually in spring (March to May) and fall (September to November) - rottweilers blow their undercoat. This is the heavy shedding period. Handfuls of soft undercoat come out with every brush stroke, and the dog looks patchy and ragged for a few weeks. The blowout typically lasts 2 to 4 weeks per season.

Female Rottweilers and Heat Cycles

Intact female rottweilers often blow coat right after a heat cycle or after weaning a litter. This can happen at any time of year and adds another layer to the shedding pattern. Spaying usually stabilizes the coat, though many spayed females still have the spring and fall blowouts.

What American Owners Use to Manage the Shedding

Most American rottweiler owners settle on a small toolkit that works for the breed. Here's the setup that gets the best results:

Brushes That Actually Work

  • Slicker brush: A medium-to-large slicker brush is the workhorse for daily maintenance. It pulls out loose undercoat without damaging the outer coat.
  • Undercoat rake: A good undercoat rake is essential for the seasonal blowout. It reaches through the outer coat to grab the dead undercoat. The FURMAX and Hertzko brands are popular with American rottweiler owners.
  • Deshedding tool: Tools like the FURminator or the DakPets Deshedding Tool are designed specifically for double coats. Used once a week, they pull out a shocking amount of loose hair. Use them gently - going too hard can cause skin irritation.
  • Rubber curry brush: Great for use in the bath. It pulls loose hair while distributing the natural oils across the coat.

Shampoo and Bathing

Rottweilers don't need frequent baths - every 6 to 8 weeks is plenty unless they roll in something smelly. Overbathing strips the natural oils and can actually increase shedding. A quality dog shampoo with oatmeal or aloe helps soothe the skin, and a deshedding shampoo before a blowout can pull out a lot of loose coat in one bath.

Vacuum and Lint Roller Strategy

Most rottweiler owners end up buying a vacuum designed for pet hair. Top picks in the US include the Dyson Animal, the Bissell Pet Hair Eraser, and the Shark Navigator with a pet tool. A good lint roller and a rubber glove (dampened and rubbed over fabric) also work wonders for clothes and furniture.

A Weekly Grooming Routine

Here's the routine that most American rottweiler owners settle into:

Daily (5 to 10 Minutes)

Quick brush with a slicker brush, especially during seasonal blowouts. A few passes over the back, sides, and tail pull out the loose hair before it ends up on your couch. Most rottweilers learn to love this and it becomes a bonding moment.

Weekly (15 to 20 Minutes)

Use the undercoat rake or deshedding tool once a week. Work in sections, going with the grain of the hair. Pay extra attention to the chest, britches (back of the thighs), and the ruff around the neck, where the undercoat is thickest.

Monthly

Nail trim, ear check, and a quick wipe-down of the face with a damp cloth. Rottweilers don't have skin folds, but they do have the classic "smush" face that can collect drool and food.

Every 6 to 8 Weeks

Bath time. Use a gentle deshedding shampoo, lather up, let it sit for 5 minutes, then rinse thoroughly. Most rottweilers do best in a bathtub or outdoor warm-weather setup. Towel dry or use a blow dryer on low heat.

Diet and Shedding

Diet makes a real difference in coat health and shedding. A rottweiler on a high-quality diet with omega-3 fatty acids sheds less and has a shinier coat than one on a cheap kibble. American vets and breeders commonly recommend:

  • Food with named meat (chicken, beef, fish) as the first ingredient.
  • Added omega-3s from fish oil, salmon oil, or flaxseed. Many owners give a daily fish oil capsule or pump.
  • Plenty of fresh water. Dehydrated skin sheds more.
  • Avoiding food with a lot of fillers (corn, wheat, soy) which some dogs are sensitive to.

Most American rottweiler owners see a noticeable difference in coat shine and shedding after 4 to 6 weeks on a better diet. If shedding seems extreme and the coat looks dull, talk to your vet about a diet change and to rule out any underlying health issues.

When Shedding Is Not Normal

Most rottweiler shedding is normal and predictable. But sometimes it's a sign of a problem. Watch for:

  • Patchy hair loss: Could be mange, ringworm, allergies, or a hot spot.
  • Bald spots with itching: Often a sign of fleas, mites, or food allergies.
  • Dandruff or flaky skin: Often a diet issue, a thyroid problem, or dry winter air.
  • Sudden heavy shedding: Could be stress, hormonal issues, or illness. Worth a vet visit if it comes on fast.
  • Hair that doesn't grow back: Could be hypothyroidism, which is more common in middle-aged rottweilers.

If the shedding looks or feels different than usual, schedule a vet visit to rule out the medical stuff before assuming it's just a seasonal blowout.

Shedding Compared to Other Popular Breeds

For American families trying to figure out how much hair to expect, here's how rottweilers stack up:

  • vs. German Shepherd: GSDs shed about the same amount year-round and have a heavier blowout.
  • vs. Labrador Retriever: Labs shed about the same amount. The hair is longer and shows up more on furniture.
  • vs. Golden Retriever: Goldens shed noticeably more, with longer hair that's harder to clean up.
  • vs. Doberman Pinscher: Dobermans shed less - they're a single coat breed.
  • vs. Standard Poodle: Poodles barely shed at all, but need professional grooming every 6 to 8 weeks.
  • vs. Bichon Frise: Bichons are very low-shedding, but the trade-off is high-maintenance grooming.

Tips for Homes With Allergies

If someone in your household has dog allergies, a rottweiler probably isn't the best fit. They produce dander, they shed year-round, and there's no such thing as a truly hypoallergenic dog. Some American allergy sufferers have mild reactions to rottweilers, while others are very sensitive. Before committing, spend time with the breed in person - at a friend's house, a breeder, or a foster through a rescue. That's the only reliable test.

If allergies are a real concern but you want a similar personality, look into standard poodles, labradoodles, or portuguese water dogs. They're all lower-shedding options with similar trainability.

Final Thoughts on Rottweiler Shedding

Do rottweilers shed? Yes, and anyone who tells you otherwise has a vacuum you should ask about. The good news is that with a few minutes of brushing most days, a weekly deeper groom, and a good vacuum cleaner, the shedding is very livable. Most American rottweiler owners settle into a routine quickly and stop noticing the hair after a few months. The trade-off - a loyal, loving, trainable, family-protective dog for a decade - is well worth a little extra cleaning.
