How Long Do Pugs Live?

Understanding pug lifespan and what you can do to maximize it

← Back to Pug Dog Names

The Short Answer: 13 to 15 Years

Most pugs live between 13 and 15 years. Some reach 16 or even 17 with excellent care. Compared to many breeds, this is a solid lifespan for a small dog. The key is managing their health proactively, especially the issues tied to their flat faces and compact bodies.

Factors That Affect Pug Lifespan

Genetics set the foundation, but lifestyle and care make a huge difference in how long your pug lives:

  • Weight management: Obesity is one of the biggest threats to pug longevity. Extra pounds strain their joints, heart, and breathing. Keeping your pug lean can add years to their life.
  • Breathing health: Pugs are brachycephalic, meaning they have shortened airways. Dogs with severe breathing issues have shorter lifespans. Choosing a breeder who prioritizes healthy airway structure helps.
  • Diet quality: High-quality food in the right portions supports overall health. Cheap fillers and overfeeding lead to obesity and inflammation.
  • Exercise: Pugs do not need marathon runs, but daily walks and play keep their weight and joints in check.
  • Veterinary care: Regular checkups catch problems early. Dental disease, in particular, is common in pugs and can affect heart and kidney health if ignored.

Common Health Issues That Impact Lifespan

Pugs are generally healthy, but they are prone to a few conditions that can shorten life if not managed:

Brachycephalic Obstructive Airway Syndrome (BOAS)

This is the most serious health concern for pugs. BOAS includes narrowed nostrils, an elongated soft palate, and a small windpipe. Affected dogs struggle to breathe, especially in heat or during exercise. Severe cases can be life-threatening and may require surgery. Avoiding obesity and keeping your pug cool are critical.

Hip Dysplasia and Joint Problems

Pugs can develop hip dysplasia and patellar luxation, where the kneecap slips out of place. These conditions cause pain and mobility issues. Maintaining a healthy weight and using joint supplements can slow progression.

Eye Conditions

Corneal ulcers, dry eye, and progressive retinal atrophy all occur in pugs. While eye issues rarely kill a dog directly, untreated infections or injuries can lead to serious complications. Regular eye care is essential.

Pug Dog Encephalitis (PDE)

This is a rare but devastating inflammatory brain disease seen only in pugs. It typically strikes young to middle-aged dogs and is fatal. There is no cure, but it affects a small percentage of the breed. Reputable breeders screen for a genetic marker associated with PDE.

Skin Fold Infections

The wrinkles on a pug's face are adorable, but they trap moisture and bacteria. Regular cleaning prevents infections that can become chronic and affect overall health.

How to Help Your Pug Live Longer

Small changes in daily care can have a big impact on lifespan:

  • Feed measured meals: Use a measuring cup and avoid free-feeding. Treats should make up no more than 10 percent of daily calories.
  • Walk daily: Two short walks per day are usually enough. Avoid exercise in hot, humid weather.
  • Keep them cool: Pugs overheat easily. Use air conditioning in summer and never leave them in a parked car.
  • Clean their face: Wipe wrinkles daily with a damp cloth to prevent bacterial buildup.
  • Brush their teeth: Dental disease is linked to heart and kidney problems. Brush several times a week or use dental chews.
  • Schedule annual bloodwork: Senior pugs especially benefit from yearly blood panels that catch organ issues early.

What to Expect as Your Pug Ages

Pugs are considered seniors around age 7. You may notice graying fur, slower movement, more napping, and hearing or vision changes. These are normal. What is not normal is sudden weight loss, labored breathing, or a dramatic change in behavior. Any of those warrant a vet visit.

Many pugs stay playful and affectionate well into their teens. With good care, your pug can be a happy companion for a decade and a half or more.

Conclusion

The typical range is 13 to 15 years, with some living even longer. Genetics matter, but your choices matter too. Keep your pug at a healthy weight, stay on top of vet care, and protect them from heat and breathing stress. A well-cared-for pug is a long-lived pug.
