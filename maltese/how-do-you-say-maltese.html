How Do You Say Maltese?

A quick, no-fuss maltese pronunciation guide for American dog owners

← Back to Maltese Puppy Names

The Short Answer

The standard American English pronunciation is mawl-TEEZ. The first syllable sounds like the word "mall," and the second syllable ends in a soft "z" sound. The plural Malteses follows the same beat, with the "ee" sound carrying the stress: mawl-TEEZ-iz. Most US dog clubs, vets, and breed guides use that pronunciation, so it is the safest pick when you are talking about the breed in everyday conversation.

Why It Matters How Do You Say Maltese

Asking how do you say maltese the right way comes up the moment you mention the breed to a vet, a groomer, or a fellow dog owner. Saying it with the right stress makes you sound like you have been around the breed for a while, and it helps you keep the conversation on the dog rather than the pronunciation. It also matters in writing — using the right form in emails, on adoption paperwork, and in social posts helps search engines and people in the breed community find you.

Breaking It Down by Syllable

American English speakers tend to break the word into two clean beats when they say it. Here is the rhythm:

  • First syllable: "mawl" — rhymes with "tall," "wall," and "call."
  • Second syllable: "TEEZ" — rhymes with "freeze," "knees," and "cheese."
  • Stress: Put the emphasis on the second syllable. The first one stays short and quick.

Slow it down for the first few tries: mawl — TEEZ. Once the rhythm feels natural, speed it up and the word will roll off the tongue like any other US dog breed name.

Common Mispronunciations to Avoid

Three mistakes come up again and again with new owners. None of them will end a conversation, but each one is worth correcting so the breed name stays consistent across the dog community.

Saying "MAL-tese" with Stress on the First Syllable

Putting the stress on the first part of the word sounds stiff and is closer to the Maltese island's original Italian pronunciation than to how American dog people use it. Aim for the second syllable to carry the weight.

Adding a Hard "A" Sound

Saying "MALT-eez" makes the first syllable sound like the grain "malt." That is a common slip, especially for owners who write the breed name a lot. Keep the "a" soft, like the vowel in "hot" or "mall."

Pronouncing the End as "TEE" or "TEES"

Cutting off the final "z" or saying a hard "s" sound at the end changes the rhythm. The correct ending in American English is a soft "z," the same sound you hear at the end of "cheese" and "please."

How Vets, Breeders, and US Dog Shows Say It

If you are still working out how do you say maltese in a real conversation, the easiest places to hear it are the Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show, the AKC National Championship, and most YouTube breed spotlights hosted by US breeders. Hosts and judges use the same two-syllable rhythm: mawl-TEEZ. Local vets in the US follow the same pattern because it is what the major kennel clubs and the Merck Veterinary Manual use in print and audio.

Plural Form and Other Quick Sayings

A handful of related phrases come up the moment you start talking about the breed. Each one follows the same core pronunciation:

  • Maltese dog: mawl-TEEZ dawg
  • Maltese puppy: mawl-TEEZ pup-ee
  • Maltese puppies: mawl-TEEZ pup-eez
  • Malteses (plural): mawl-TEEZ-iz
  • Maltese mix: mawl-TEEZ miks

Once the basic rhythm is locked in, the rest of the phrases fall into place without extra effort.

Where the Word Comes From

The breed takes its name from the island nation of Malta in the central Mediterranean. The original Maltese adjective in the local language is "Malti," which leans on the first syllable. When the name moved into English through 19th-century kennel clubs, the stress shifted to the second syllable, and the soft "z" ending settled in. That is the version American English speakers use today, and it is what you will hear on every US dog show broadcast.

Pronouncing Related Words

Once you have the breed name down, a few related terms are worth knowing for a vet visit or a trip to the groomer:

  • Maltese Shih Tzu mix (Malshi): MAWL-shee
  • Maltese Yorkie mix (Morkie): MORE-kee
  • Maltese Bichon mix (Maltichon): MAWL-tih-kon
  • Maltese Poodle mix (Maltipoo): MAWL-tee-poo

Most of these designer names follow the same first-syllable sound as the parent breed, so the rhythm you just practiced carries over.

A 30-Second Practice Drill

Here is a quick drill you can run through once or twice before the next vet visit:

  1. Say "mall" out loud, then "mall, mall, mall."
  2. Say "cheese" out loud, then "cheese, cheese, cheese."
  3. Now put them together without a pause: "mawl — TEEZ."
  4. Add the ending for the plural: "mawl — TEEZ — iz."
  5. Drop the rhythm into a sentence: "Our mawl-TEEZ loves the dog park."

Run that three times and the word will feel automatic the next time it comes up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it "MALT-eez" or "mawl-TEEZ"? American English prefers "mawl-TEEZ" with the stress on the second syllable.

How do British speakers say it? The British version is similar, with the second syllable carrying the stress, but the vowel is sometimes tighter. Both are correct in their own region.

How do I say the plural? The most common US plural is "Malteses," pronounced mawl-TEEZ-iz.

Final Word

The maltese pronunciation is short, simple, and consistent across the US dog world: mawl-TEEZ, with the second syllable doing the work. Nail that rhythm, and the rest of the breed's vocabulary falls into place without a second thought. The next time someone asks you how do you say maltese, you will have a clear answer ready.
