How Long Do Malteses Live?

A clear look at the maltese lifespan and what shapes it for American owners

The Short Answer

Most malteses live between 12 and 15 years. Many American owners report their dogs reaching 16 or 17 with good care, while a smaller group passes earlier from genetic issues. Toy breeds in general tend to outlive larger dogs, and the maltese is on the longer end of that scale. If you are asking how long do malteses live because you are planning to bring one home, plan for at least a dozen years of daily walks, vet visits, and grooming appointments.

Average Maltese Lifespan by the Numbers

The American Kennel Club lists the maltese lifespan at 12 to 15 years. A 2022 UK study of over 50 dog breeds put the maltese median age at 12.5 years, which lines up with what most US vets see in the exam room. A well-bred dog from a health-tested parent usually lands in the middle of that range. Random-bred or puppy-mill dogs often sit on the lower end because of weaker immune systems and a higher rate of congenital problems.

Life Stages of a Maltese

  • Puppy (0 to 12 months): Fast growth, teething, and the bulk of house training. Most US vets schedule vaccines at 8, 12, and 16 weeks.
  • Young adult (1 to 4 years): Energy peaks here. A healthy maltese is usually fully grown by 12 months and weighs under seven pounds.
  • Adult (4 to 9 years): Calmer, easier to manage, and a good window for routine bloodwork at the annual checkup.
  • Senior (9 to 15+ years): Slower walks, more naps, and twice-yearly vet visits are common in this stage.

What Affects How Long a Maltese Lives

Genetics, daily care, and plain luck all play a role. A few factors stand out for American households:

Diet and Weight

Maltese puppies are tiny, and it is easy to overfeed them. Keeping a maltese at a steady four to seven pounds puts less stress on the heart and joints. US brands that meet AAFCO standards for small breeds are a safe baseline, and most owners feed twice a day once the puppy is past six months.

Dental Health

Small breeds are notorious for dental crowding. Plaque buildup can lead to heart and kidney trouble if it goes untreated. Brushing a few times a week, plus an annual cleaning at the vet, is the standard advice from American veterinary dentists.

Heart and Liver Screening

Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) and portosystemic shunts show up in the breed more than people expect. Reputable breeders run cardiac and liver panels on the parents. If you are buying a puppy, ask to see the results rather than just the paperwork.

Patellar Luxation

Loose kneecaps are common in toy breeds. Mild cases do not shorten a maltese lifespan, but severe cases may need surgery to keep the dog mobile into the senior years.

How to Add Healthy Years

Daily habits matter more than supplements. A steady routine of two short walks, a measured diet, clean teeth, and yearly bloodwork tends to pay off in the long run. Keep the dog at a healthy weight, avoid stairs when possible, and watch for any sudden behavior change. Catching a small issue early almost always costs less and adds more years than waiting until something is obviously wrong.

Comparing to Other Small Breeds

Side by side with similar US household favorites, the maltese sits in the top half for longevity:

  • Maltese: 12 to 15 years
  • Yorkshire Terrier: 11 to 15 years
  • Pomeranian: 12 to 16 years
  • Bichon Frise: 12 to 15 years
  • Chihuahua: 12 to 20 years

So when an owner asks how long do malteses live, the honest answer is that the breed does better than most medium and large dogs, but it is not the longest-living toy breed in the room.

Common Signs of Aging

Owners usually notice a few changes around year nine or ten. The coat may thin, the muzzle grays, and stairs get harder. Eyes can develop a slight cloudiness that does not always need treatment. Appetite often stays steady, but weight can creep up if the walks shorten. A vet visit every six months from age eight onward catches kidney, heart, and thyroid shifts before they become serious.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do malteses live longer than other dogs? Yes, they live longer than most medium and large breeds, and they are in line with other toy breeds.

What is the oldest maltese on record? Several maltese have reached 18 to 20 years in US homes, though these are exceptions rather than the rule.

Does spaying or neutering change the lifespan? Most US vets recommend spaying or neutering, and the procedure is linked to a lower rate of certain cancers when done at the right age.

Final Word

For most American families, how long do malteses live comes down to the 12-to-15-year window. Good breeding, steady weight, clean teeth, and regular vet care push a dog toward the upper end of that range.
