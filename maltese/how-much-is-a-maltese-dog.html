How Much Is a Maltese Dog?

A practical 2026 price breakdown for American buyers and owners

The Quick Answer

In the United States, a maltese puppy from a reputable breeder usually costs between $1,500 and $4,000. Show-line puppies from champion bloodlines can climb past $6,000. Adoption through a breed rescue typically runs from $300 to $600 and usually covers spay or neuter, vaccines, and a microchip. If you are budgeting for a new dog, expect the first year to cost more than later years because of one-time setup, training, and grooming gear.

What Drives the Price of a Maltese Puppy

Several things push the price tag up or down for American buyers. Knowing what to look for helps you avoid paying too much, or too little, for the same dog.

Breeder Reputation and Health Testing

Breeders who run cardiac, patellar, and liver panels on the parents charge more because the testing itself costs money. A pup from health-tested parents usually sells for $2,000 to $4,000. A breeder who skips these tests and still asks for top dollar is a red flag.

Location in the US

Coastal cities tend to price higher. In Los Angeles, New York, and Boston, $3,000 to $4,500 is a common range. The Midwest and the South usually sit a bit lower, with $1,500 to $2,500 being more typical.

Coat and Color

Standard white is the most common and the most affordable. Pure white with a perfect silk coat commands a premium. The breed standard only allows white, so any breeder offering "rare" colors is usually breeding outside the standard and charging extra for it.

Age of the Dog

Younger puppies cost more than older ones. A 4-month-old will be near the top of the range, while a 9-to-12-month-old might be listed for less because the small-breed puppy window has passed.

Real Price Ranges for 2026

  • Pet-quality puppy, health-tested parents: $1,500 to $3,000
  • Show-quality puppy from a champion line: $3,500 to $6,000
  • Older puppy or young adult from a breeder: $1,000 to $2,000
  • Breed rescue adoption: $300 to $600
  • General shelter (mixed breed): $100 to $400

These are the kinds of numbers US owners post in breed forums and on regional kennel club sites. Always confirm what is included before you wire a deposit.

Yearly Cost of Owning a Maltese

The purchase price is just the start. Here is what most American owners spend each year to keep a maltese healthy and clean.

Food

Small-breed kibble that meets AAFCO standards runs about $25 to $50 a month. A yearly estimate is $300 to $600.

Grooming

Maltese coats need regular care. A professional groomer visit every 4 to 6 weeks averages $60 to $90 in most US cities. That is around $800 to $1,200 a year for full grooming, or less if you keep the coat trimmed short at home.

Vet Care

Annual exams, vaccines, heartworm prevention, and flea control add up to $400 to $700 a year for a healthy adult. Seniors and dogs with chronic issues cost more.

Dental Cleaning

Small breeds need professional dental work under anesthesia every 1 to 2 years. Each visit runs $300 to $700 in most US clinics.

Boarding, Daycare, and Pet Sitting

When work or travel pulls an owner away, a weekend of boarding in a mid-size US city costs $50 to $90 a night. Plan on a few hundred dollars a year if you travel often.

Toys, Beds, and Misc

Small-dog toys wear out fast, and a comfy bed is non-negotiable. A reasonable yearly budget here is $150 to $300.

First-Year Total in the US

Add it all up for a new owner buying a $2,500 puppy and living in a mid-size US city:

  • Purchase price: $2,500
  • Initial vet visit and vaccines: $200 to $400
  • Spay or neuter: $300 to $600
  • Crate, bed, bowls, leash, brushes: $200 to $400
  • Training classes: $150 to $300
  • Food, toys, and supplies for the year: $400 to $700

A reasonable first-year budget is $4,000 to $5,500. After the first year, the steady-state cost usually lands between $1,800 and $2,800 a year.

Red Flags When Shopping

Some quick checks save a lot of money and heartbreak. Avoid any seller who:

  • Refuses to let you visit where the puppies are raised
  • Sells puppies under 8 weeks old
  • Has no health testing on the parents
  • Offers multiple breeds at the same address
  • Pushes for payment through apps or wire transfers only

A reputable breeder will ask you questions, share vet records, and stay in touch after the sale. That kind of relationship is worth the higher upfront price.

Adoption vs Buying

Rescue groups dedicated to the breed exist across the US, including groups in California, Texas, Florida, and the Northeast. Adoption fees cover spay or neuter, vaccines, and microchipping, so the value is strong. Older malteses in rescue often skip the teething and house-training stage, which fits owners who do not want to deal with a puppy phase.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a maltese dog at a pet store? Pet store puppies usually list for $2,500 to $5,000. Most come from large-scale breeding operations, so ask for the breeder's name and verify health testing before buying.

How much is a maltese dog from a rescue? Breed rescues charge $300 to $600, and the dog usually arrives spayed or neutered, vaccinated, and microchipped.

How much is a teacup maltese? Teacop is a marketing term, not an official size. Smaller-than-standard pups from health-tested parents are rare and usually cost more than the standard range.

Final Word

So how much is a maltese dog in the US? Plan on $1,500 to $4,000 for a well-bred puppy, $300 to $600 for a rescue, and $1,800 to $2,800 a year after that for food, grooming, and vet care. The price tag is fair for a long-lived companion that fits in a studio apartment and gets along with most American households.
